# Concordance — prior-art and fit scan (2026-10-22)

Framing: Concordance = (deterministic verifier → verdict + worked trail + SHA-256 seal, hash-chained ledger) + "scribe" loop (N writers → writers×claims matrix → SVD disagreement axis → deterministic break → feedback → converge). Area A attacks the second half; Area B attacks the first.

## A. Consensus / ensemble / debate

**Correlated error (the failure mode) — measured:**
- [Kim et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962): 350+ models; when two models both err they give the *same* wrong answer 60% of the time; correlation rises with size/accuracy, across providers.
- [Bradley "Madness of Crowds"](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01539v2): 37 models on MMLU-Pro, median z-score 13.15 for same-wrong-answer; clusters by vendor.
- [Kohli "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes" (Apple, May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29800): 9 judges / 7 families ≈ 2.0–2.5 effective independent votes; Condorcet gap up to 22 pts; panel 72.0% vs best single judge 71.8%.
- [Chen "Co-Failure Ceiling" (Jun 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27288): 67 models / 21 providers; all-wrong rate β = 0.052 math, 0.079 code, 0.127 free-response GPQA; "gains come from models failing on different questions, not from adding more models."
- [Kuai et al. behavioral entanglement audit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07650): 18 models; entanglement-aware reweighting 0.882 vs MV 0.847.

**Consensus + deterministic checker:** nobody I found combines a multi-writer matrix with a deterministic breaker as a product. Closest are the LLM+formal-verifier loops ([4/δ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02080), [ZenML's "deterministic verification layer" case](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/deterministic-verification-layer-for-ai-coding-agents)) and the [cross-model review method](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/cross-model-review-consensus-verification-2026) that routes split verdicts to a deterministic check.

Every item in B attests *that a model/agent produced X*. None attest *that X is true*. Concordance is the only thing in this scan whose seal binds a *verdict + worked trail*, and that is both its differentiation and its liability: the seal is only as good as the verifier's domain coverage, and a self-signed, private ledger is weaker than a Rekor/SCITT inclusion proof.

## (a) Five closest comparables, ranked
1. [LLMs as a Jury (Liu 2026)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10139) — same multi-writer consensus idea, names the shared-error floor Concordance claims to break.
2. [Signet](https://mcpservers.org/id/servers/prismer-ai/signet) + [SCITT agent-receipt draft](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-01.html) — the ledger half, nearly feature-identical, minus verdicts.
3. [4/δ LLM-verifier loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02080) — the "deterministic verifier breaks, writer retries, converges" loop with a termination proof.
4. [DiscoUQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20975v1) / [Minority Sentinel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29270) — structured-disagreement analysis over agent ensembles.
5. [Together MoA](https://docs.together.ai/docs/mixture-of-agents) / [Sakana TreeQuest](https://venturebeat.com/ai/sakana-ais-treequest-deploy-multi-model-teams-that-outperform-individual-llms-by-30) — shipped multi-model orchestration without verification.

## (b) Five best fits (integrate, don't compete)
1. **SCITT**: wrap each seal as a COSE Signed Statement per [draft-noa](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-01.html); register to a transparency service for third-party inclusion proofs.
2. **Sigstore/Rekor**: publish seal hashes as in-toto Statements signed keylessly ([guide](https://www.fast.io/resources/ai-agent-output-attestation.md)); fixes the "self-attested ledger" weakness for free.
3. **C2PA sidecar manifests**: emit a manifest with a custom `concordance.verdict` assertion so Content Credentials viewers display verified-vs-unverified; text requires sidecar.
4. **LangGraph node / AutoGen tool / MCP server**: ship `verify()` as the deterministic arbiter inside existing debate/MoA graphs ([frameworks](https://www.spheron.network/blog/langgraph-vs-crewai-vs-autogen-2026/)); the [Narrow Highway MCP](https://narrowhighway.com) already exists, so this is packaging, not engineering.
5. **EigenAI / zkTLS notarization of writer outputs**: pair "model produced X" ([EigenAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00182v1), [Opacity](https://unpkg.com/@layr-labs/agentkit-opacity@0.1.1/README.md)) with Concordance's "X is true" so the seal covers both halves.

## (c) Strongest prior art for "SVD over multi-agent outputs to locate disagreement"
No direct hit. The nearest lineage is spectral crowdsourcing: [Zhang, Chen, Zhou, Jordan "Spectral Methods meet EM" (NIPS 2014)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/papers/zhang-et-al-nips15) and [Parisi et al. "Ranking and combining multiple predictors without labeled data" (PNAS 2014)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.3257) — leading eigenvector of the worker-covariance matrix recovers worker reliabilities. In the LLM era, [Kohli 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29800) uses the top eigenvalue of judges' φ-matrix as n_eff (2.16), and [DiscoUQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20975v1) uses a per-question PCA variance ratio. Nobody factorizes a writers×claims matrix to isolate the disagreement axis and then feeds that axis to a deterministic checker. A patent search on "spectral decomposition of annotator-item matrix" (Parisi/Nadler) is warranted before claiming novelty; the application to claims-plus-verifier looks clear.

## (d) "Models agree 99% — what is consensus buying?"
Honest answer: **little, unless a verifier is in the loop.**
- Agreement is weakly predictive of correctness: ρ = 0.20–0.59 over 265k samples; GPT-4.1 at ≥0.8 self-agreement on GPQA was wrong 48% of the time ([Ding 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08065)).
- Nine judges ≈ two votes; panel beat best single judge by 0.2 pts ([Kohli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29800)).
- All-wrong floor 5–13% depending on format ([Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27288)); when both err, same wrong answer 60% ([Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)).
- Debate rarely beats self-consistency ([Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08788v3)); best aggregation tricks add ~1 pt ([Ai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01499)).
- Minority is right in ~25% of splits, but exploiting it nets +1.7% ([Minority Sentinel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29270)).

So consensus buys (1) a 5–13% residual the ensemble cannot see, and (2) a *localization* signal: disagreement tells you *where* to spend deterministic verification. That is the defensible pitch — SVD as a triage index for the verifier, not as the judge. Claim it that way; the "99% agree" number itself argues against consensus-as-truth, and the data above is on the other side of that argument.
